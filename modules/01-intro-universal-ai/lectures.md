## Official Course Content

### Lecture 1: Artificial Intelligence Basics

#### Overview
Taught by Professor Georgios Stamou (National Technical University of Athens, Visiting Professor at MIT).  
This lecture traces AI’s evolution from early logical reasoning (Aristotle, Boole, Turing) to symbolic AI and expert systems, and then to today’s data‑driven deep learning models.  

Key theme: **complexity** — real‑world problems create enormous search spaces, so AI depends on heuristics (patterns and shortcuts learned from rules or data) to guide decisions efficiently.  

---

#### Learning Objectives
By the end of this lecture, learners will be able to:
- Understand what an AI agent is and how perception, thinking, and action work together.  
- Recognize the difference between symbolic AI and modern machine learning, and why the field shifted toward data‑driven methods.  
- Explain why brute‑force problem solving fails and how heuristics help AI handle huge search spaces.  
- Describe how neural networks learn from data, including training, inference, and generalization.  
- Identify common AI tasks such as classification, regression, translation, and question answering.  

---

#### Key Concepts
- **AI Agents**: Defined by perception, thinking, and action in a continuous feedback loop.  
- **Historical Foundations**: Aristotle’s syllogisms, Boole’s algebra of logic, Turing’s computation theory, Dartmouth Conference (1956).  
- **Symbolic AI & Expert Systems**: MYCIN (medical diagnosis), SHRDLU (natural language). Narrow but foundational.  
- **Shift to Data‑Driven AI**: Explosion of data + computing power → deep neural networks and foundation models.  
- **Brute‑Force Search vs Heuristics**: Brute force impractical; heuristics guide efficient problem solving. Example: AlphaFold solving protein folding.  
- **Learning & Decision Making**: Classification, regression, translation, question answering. Training → inference → generalization.

### Lecture 2: AI in Action

#### Overview
This lecture focuses on how AI models operate in practice, solving problems by interacting with the world. It explains how AI agents perceive, reason, and act, and how they apply learned patterns to real‑world tasks.  

---

#### Key Concepts
- **AI Agent Architecture**: Input (world state) → Model (reasoning) → Output (action). Continuous loop of perception, reasoning, and action.  
- **Common AI Tasks**:  
  - Binary classification (positive/negative sentiment).  
  - Regression (predicting hospital stay length).  
  - Multi‑class classification (diagnosing pneumonia vs bronchitis vs asthma).  
  - Question answering (prompt‑based responses).  
  - Translation (text from one language to another).  
  - Generative AI (creating text, images, sound, video).  

- **Brute‑Force Search Limitations**:  
  - Sliding‑tile puzzles show exponential growth in search states.  
  - Real‑world problems (medical diagnosis, protein folding) have astronomical search spaces.  
  - Brute force is impossible; heuristics are essential.  

- **Heuristics**:  
  - Symbolic (expert rules).  
  - Statistical (patterns learned from data).  
  - Guide models toward promising solutions efficiently.  

- **How AI Models Learn**:  
  - Training on datasets (supervised, unsupervised, reinforcement).  
  - Inference: applying learned patterns to new inputs.  
  - Generalization: handling unseen data.  
  - Deep neural networks: layers of neurons learning increasingly abstract representations.  
  - Foundation models: trillions of parameters, powering systems like ChatGPT.
