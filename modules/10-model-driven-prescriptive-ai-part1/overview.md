## 🌟 Executive Summary

Welcome to **Model-Driven Prescriptive AI Part 1**. While predictive AI models generate forecasts and generative AI models synthesize content, **prescriptive AI** focuses on the downstream layer of decision-making: optimizing complex, interconnected choices across real-world systems under capacity, resource, and operational constraints.

This module introduces the mathematical foundations and computational implementation of **optimization models**. Learners move from foundational calculus and linear programming principles to large-scale network optimization and humanitarian operations research.

---

## 🎯 Key Learning Goals

By completing this module, we have achieved the following core capabilities:

1. **Optimization Foundations**: Defined decision variables ($x$), objective functions ($f(x)$), constraints ($g_i(x) \le 0$), feasible regions, and optimal solutions.
2. **Optimization Taxonomy**: Distinguished between **Linear Optimization (LP)**, **Integer/Binary Optimization (IP/MIP)**, and **Nonlinear Optimization (NLP)**, recognizing their respective computational characteristics and application domains.
3. **Prediction vs. Prescription**: Understood how optimization underpins model fitting in predictive AI (loss function minimization) and downstream action selection in prescriptive AI.
4. **Airline Network Revenue Management**: Formulated and solved multi-flight, multi-fare-class network capacity allocation models, deriving shadow prices (dual values) to surpass greedy heuristic booking limits.
5. **Network Optimization**: Formulated Shortest Path, Assignment (rideshare platform matching), and Minimum Cost Network Flow models using node balance constraints ($\sum f_{\text{in}} - \sum f_{\text{out}} = d_i$) and total unimodularity.
6. **Analytics of Zero Hunger**: Extended network flow models to multi-commodity flows and nutritionally-constrained diet optimization for the World Food Programme (WFP), balancing procurement, transportation, and health requirements.
