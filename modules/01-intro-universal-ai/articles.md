### Article 1: How AI Works

#### Overview & Learning Objectives
This article introduces the fundamentals of AI, focusing on definitions, distinctions between rule‑based and machine learning systems, and the importance of training data.  

By the end of this lesson, learners should be able to:
- Distinguish between rule‑based and machine learning AI systems.  
- Understand common training methods for machine learning.  
- Explain why engineers split data into training, validation, and test sets.  
- Explain how generative AI models create text, images, and other content.  

---

#### What is AI?
Artificial intelligence is the use of computers to make automated decisions or predictions. Most AI systems perform a single, defined task — such as searching databases or identifying patterns in images.  

John McCarthy coined the term “artificial intelligence” in 1955, defining it as *“the science and engineering of making intelligent machines.”*  

AI overlaps with other technologies:
- Autonomous robots use AI to interpret environments.  
- Big data applications rely on AI for analysis.  
- Cybersecurity systems use AI to detect attacks.  
- Virtual reality products use AI to track and map user movements.  

---

#### How AI Works
AI is not a single technology but a category of them. Common applications include:
- Forecasting trends and events.  
- Computer vision (interpreting visual data).  
- Natural language processing (interpreting language).  
- Generative AI (creating new content).  

Modern AI systems fall into two categories:
1. **Rule‑based systems** — follow explicit human‑defined rules (e.g., spam filters).  
2. **Machine learning systems** — learn from examples and adapt over time.  

---

#### Machine Learning Training Techniques
- **Supervised Learning**: Uses labeled data for classification or regression tasks.  
- **Unsupervised Learning**: Uses unlabeled data to discover patterns.  
- **Reinforcement Learning**: Learns through trial and error over time, common in gaming and adaptive decision‑making.  

---

#### Deep Learning & Neural Networks
Deep learning uses neural networks with many layers to interpret complex data. Each neuron processes inputs and passes outputs to the next layer.  

Applications include:
- Facial recognition  
- Language translation  
- Autonomous vehicles  
- Generative AI (e.g., ChatGPT)  

Challenges:
- Opaque decision‑making (“black box” problem).  
- Bias in training data.  
- High energy and computational costs.

### Article 2: AI in Action

#### Overview
This article explores how AI is applied in real-world contexts, highlighting both its transformative potential and the challenges it introduces.  

---

#### Applications of AI
AI is now ubiquitous across industries:
- **Search & Recommendation Engines** — powering platforms like Google, YouTube, and Netflix.  
- **Spam Filters & Cybersecurity** — detecting threats and filtering unwanted content.  
- **Healthcare** — diagnosing diseases, predicting patient outcomes, and supporting medical imaging.  
- **Transportation** — self-driving cars, lane-keeping systems, and traffic optimization.  
- **Finance** — fraud detection, credit scoring, and algorithmic trading.  
- **Creative Industries** — generative AI producing text, images, music, and code.  

---

#### Human + AI Collaboration
- AI is increasingly used to **assist rather than replace** workers.  
- Examples include doctors using AI for diagnostics, or teachers using AI tools to personalize learning.  
- This shift highlights AI’s role as an augmentation tool, not just automation.  

---

#### Controversies & Challenges
- **Facial Recognition Backlash** — bans and regulations due to privacy and bias concerns.  
- **Generative AI Lawsuits** — disputes over copyright, data usage, and labor impacts.  
- **Bias & Fairness** — AI systems can reinforce inequalities if trained on biased data.  
- **Transparency Issues** — deep learning models often act as “black boxes,” making it hard to explain decisions.  

---

#### Why AI Matters Now
AI has moved from research labs into mainstream life. Its relevance today is driven by:
- **Explosion of Data** — mobile devices, sensors, and digital platforms generate massive datasets.  
- **Advances in Hardware** — faster processors and cheaper storage enable larger models.  
- **Cloud Computing** — scalable infrastructure allows organizations to train and deploy AI globally.  
- **Generative AI** — systems like ChatGPT demonstrate AI’s ability to create content, sparking both excitement and concern.
